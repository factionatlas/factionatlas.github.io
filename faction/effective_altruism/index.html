Effective altruism (EA)

LAYER ALIGNMENT · ERA 2011–present · FIRST SEEN 2026-11-21

Active

Idea faction, outside the right. In its own words, effective altruism "Effective altruism aims to find the best ways to do good, and put those findings into practice." (What is effective altruism?[1]). (CEA guiding principles[2]). [4].

CEA's stated principles are commitment to others, a scientific mindset, openness, integrity and collaborative spirit. CEA also says the movement is not defined by any single organisation or person, is not the same as longtermism, and is not the same as rationalism or as utilitarianism (CEA press kit[5]). Short history: GiveWell was founded in 2007 by Holden Karnofsky and Elie Hassenfeld (Wikipedia: GiveWell[8]).

Giving What We Can and the 10% Pledge were created in 2009; the name 'effective altruism' was coined in 2011, when the Centre for Effective Altruism was founded as an umbrella for Giving What We Can and 80,000 Hours, which was also founded that year (CEA press kit[5]; GWWC about[6]; 80,000 Hours about[7]).

GiveWell Labs became the Open Philanthropy Project in 2014 and later Open Philanthropy; on 18 November 2025 the organisation renamed itself Coefficient Giving, writing: "Today, Open Philanthropy is becoming Coefficient Giving." Its mission, in its own words, "Our mission is to help others as much as we can with the resources available to us." (announcement[9]; home[13]; history[14]).

On 11 November 2022, FTX, Alameda Research and over 100 affiliates filed for bankruptcy and Sam Bankman-Fried resigned as FTX CEO (Wikipedia: Bankruptcy of FTX[11]). He had publicly identified with effective altruism and earning to give; after the collapse he told Axios, "The thing that was bullshit was not EA. EA, I happen to care about deeply." (Axios, 30 Nov 2022[15]).

The FTX Future Fund team resigned, and EA organisations issued statements (Wikipedia: Bankman-Fried[16]; 80,000 Hours FTX statement / advice changes[17]).
