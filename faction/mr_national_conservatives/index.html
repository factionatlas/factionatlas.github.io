National conservatives (NatCon)

LAYER ALIGNMENT · FIRST SEEN 2026-11-01

Also listed as: MR: National conservatives (NatCon)

Active

National conservatives describe themselves as "conservatives" who "emphasize the idea of the nation", seeing "a world of independent nations" as "the only genuine alternative to universalist ideologies now seeking to impose a homogenizing, locality-destroying imperium over the entire globe."

Their founding text is "National Conservatism: A Statement of Principles" (published 15 June 2022, statement[1]), drafted by Will Chamberlain, Christopher DeMuth, Rod Dreher, Yoram Hazony, Daniel McCarthy, Joshua Mitchell, N.S. Lyons, John O'Sullivan and R.R. Reno "on behalf of the Edmund Burke Foundation".

It opposes "transferring the authority of elected governments to transnational or supranational bodies" and rejects both the "imperialism of China, Russia, and other authoritarian powers" and "the liberal imperialism of the last generation"; calls for "a strong but limited state" and "a drastic reduction in the scope of the administrative state"; holds that "Where a Christian majority exists, public life should be rooted in Christianity and its moral vision" while "Jews and other religious minorities are to be protected"; says "the free market cannot be absolute. Economic policy must serve the general welfare of the nation"; calls "The traditional family, built around a lifelong bond between a man and a woman" the foundation of civilization; asks for "much more restrictive" immigration policies that "may sometimes include a moratorium"; and states "No person's worth or loyalties can be judged by the shape of his features, the color of his skin, or the results of a lab test."

The statement is signed by individuals whose affiliations are "for identification purposes only"; signing it is the closest thing to a self-test, and there is no membership. Reason's review of Laura Field's Furious Minds counts "national conservatives (such as Yoram Hazony...)" as one of three camps of the New Right, beside Claremonters and postliberals (review[2]).

Atlas notes: Hazony's bio: 'The Virtue of Nationalism... Host @NatConTalk | Trustee @Heritage'; he says 'Liberalism is perfectly capable of justifying a dictatorship of the "Enlightened."' (post[12]) and promoted the NatCon Jerusalem talk 'Biblical Nationalism is Not for Israel Alone' (post[13]).

Heritage president Kevin Roberts: 'Globalism has failed. Markets don't magically transform people into Americans' and welcomes the VP 'speaking openly about restoring American nationalism' (post[14]). Small accounts self-label too: 'a national conservative in the America First tradition' (post[15]); a Hungarian analyst: 'I am a national conservative as far as domestic politics go and a hard-core realist' (post[16]).

Rivals and critics: Cato says 'NatCons... want to use government power to impose their nationalist agenda, abandoning free markets and limited government' (post[3]); a founding-principles (Jaffa/Claremont) writer contrasts Jaffa with Deneen and worries about NatCon's view of the founding (post[17]); Reference: a pro-Israel critic lumps 'The Groyper/NatCon movement' together (post[18]), but NatCon's Jerusalem conference and Hazony's ties to Israel make it distinct from the groypers.

Speaker lists (Hawley, Vance, Rubio, DeSantis, Thiel, Roberts, Tucker Carlson, Orban, Meloni) are co-appearance only, not membership.
