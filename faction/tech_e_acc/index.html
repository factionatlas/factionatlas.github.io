Effective accelerationism (e/acc)

LAYER ALIGNMENT · ERA 2022–present · FIRST SEEN 2026-11-10

Also listed as: TECH: Effective accelerationism (e/acc)

Active

Idea faction. Wikipedia: 'Effective accelerationism (e/acc) is a 21st-century ideological movement that advocates for an explicitly pro-technology stance. Its proponents believe that unrestricted technological progress, especially driven by artificial intelligence, is a solution to universal human problems, such as poverty, war, and climate change.'

Its proponents label opponents 'doomers' or 'decels'. The name is a portmanteau of 'effective altruism' and 'accelerationism'; the earliest known reference is a May 2022 newsletter by four pseudonymous X accounts including @BasedBeffJezos, a persona that Forbes identified in December 2023 as Guillaume Verdon.

Investors Marc Andreessen and Garry Tan endorsed it by adding 'e/acc' to their profiles; Andreessen's 2023 'Techno-Optimist Manifesto' is described as espousing its views. RIVAL: effective altruism. Wikipedia: e/acc 'diverges from the principles of effective altruism' mainly over AI existential risk, which effective altruists want handled cautiously and e/acc proponents generally consider negligible.

Intellectual lineage: Beff Jezos and bayes, in 'Notes on e/acc principles and tenets' (10 Jul 2022), wrote: "Viewing Capitalism as a form of intelligence was also something that Nick Land would emphasize in his original founding text for accelerationism." They link that 'original founding text' to Nick Land's 'Meltdown' (tenets[2]; Land text they linked:[7]).

The inaugural e/acc post (authored by @zestular, @creatine_cycle, @BasedBeffJezos and @bayeslord) opens with "Accelerationism is simply the self-awareness of capitalism, which has scarcely begun. (“We haven’t seen anything yet.”)" without naming Land in that post (inaugural/repost[3]).

Yarvin, Moldbug and neoreaction are not mentioned in those founding posts, nor in Verdon's later Lex Fridman (#407) or Atlas Society interviews.

We’re partly joking, partly serious." and contrasts the two: "Unlike EA, e/acc" (not technocratic, not passively risk-averse like 'EAs that “wish everything would just slow down”', not human-centric, and rejecting 'the desire for a panopticon implied by longtermist EA beliefs'). Co-author @zestular posted, 'e/acc is EA, but based.' The tenets post also calls over-regulation "over-regulating technologies suppresses variance and hence slows down progress towards higher utility technologies and advancement of civilization, a contrast to anti-AGI factions of EA". Verdon later said, "We had to start the resistance to that movement.

That's originally how E/Acc started.

It was antithetical to EA in some ways" (Atlas Society interview[5]), and on Lex, "To me, having a movement that is opposite to what was the mainstream narrative that was taking over Silicon Valley was important to shift the dynamic range of opinions." He described EA as having "it provided initially a sense of community for engineers, and intellectuals, and rationalists in the early days", not as his own prior home (Lex transcript[4]).
